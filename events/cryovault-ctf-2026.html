48 writeups

  • leaky-evalRing-LWE decryption leak crypto The service publishes one exact noise coefficient per Ring-LWE sample. Each leak is a full linear equation over Z_Q, so 72 samples solve for the ternary secret by plain Gaussian elimination and open the AES-GCM flag.
  • hidden-weightsorthogonal relation lattice crypto Both the subset-sum weights and the 0/1 selection matrix are hidden, so the usual embedding is useless. LLL on the relation lattice exposes 40 short kernel vectors that pin down the secret rows, and a linear solve mod M recovers the weights.
  • free-lunchCICO on a cube-root permutation crypto A 20-round cube-root permutation over GF(p)^2 must map (x, 0) to an output ending in zero. Written as a backward recurrence, the degree only triples every two steps, so the whole problem collapses to roots of one degree-59049 polynomial.
  • implicit-twinsimplicit factorization of two RSA moduli crypto Two 1280-bit moduli share a long run of low bits in their 1024-bit primes. Because the other primes are only 256 bits, the short vector (q1, q2) in a 2-D lattice falls out of plain Gauss reduction and factors both.
  • half-truthsReed-Solomon list decoding crypto 512 Shamir shares with only 185 honest, far past what any unique decoder can fix. Sudan-style list decoding with a carefully chosen degree bound recovers the polynomial, and a de-branched root finder pulls out the coefficients.
  • toxic-wasteCheon's attack on a trimmed KZG setup crypto Keeping only the first and last powers of tau looked like a space saving. Cheon's attack turns those two group elements into two baby-step giant-step phases of about 2^22 steps each, recovering tau and the flag key.
  • sparse-changeProny's problem over a finite field crypto Only 48 evaluations of a secret 24-term sparse polynomial are given. The samples form a sum of exponentials, so a Hankel system finds the recurrence, Cantor-Zassenhaus splits it, and Pohlig-Hellman turns the roots into exponents.
  • one-morepolynomial-time ROS attack on blind Schnorr crypto A blind Schnorr signing oracle that lets you open 256 concurrent sessions. The polynomial-time ROS attack forges one more valid signature than sessions answered in a few seconds, with no Wagner tree and no heavy memory.
  • The-RecurrenceLucas sequence and discrete log rev The 28-byte input is a 224-bit scalar fed through a Lucas V-sequence ladder. The check reduces to a quadratic in alpha^k, and the group order is smooth enough for Pohlig-Hellman to recover the scalar in milliseconds.
  • The-Loomsecond-order reversible automaton rev A million rounds of a nonlinear update look expensive, but the recurrence P[n+1] = g(P[n]) xor P[n-1] is its own inverse. Walking backward from the two exposed states recovers the input.
  • the-pressinverting an LZMA-style range coder rev 32 bytes in, 36 bytes out, with hand-rolled arithmetic coding after an XOR-diff pass. Running the matching range decoder over the target and then undoing the prefix XOR gives the input.
  • Palimpestbitcode VM solved with SMT rev A Zig-built VM re-keys itself from its own register state on every one of 320 steps. Rather than invert it by hand, the whole loop is symbolised for bitwuzla with printable-ASCII constraints and solved in one query.
  • OUROBOROSJIT code with a self-referential key rev The binary synthesises its own checker at runtime, one block per 8 input bytes. The fixed function prologue is free known plaintext against the keystream, so each block's seed and word follow with no 2^64 search.
  • The Ledgersubset-sum via lattice reduction rev The check is an exact integer subset sum over 128 entries of 400 bits, a density of about 0.32. BKZ with block size 20 on the standard low-density lattice yields the 128-bit selector, which is the input.
  • Turning Chartinverting a fixed 320-round program rev Every round's operation comes from a PRNG that never sees the input, so the whole thing is a fixed invertible byte program. Logging the ops under gdb and applying their inverses in reverse order recovers the flag.
  • Ghost in the Spoolerspooler shadow file and tracking dots forensics The flag hashes a username, printer serial and timestamp. The .SHD file gives the owner and FILETIME, and the faint yellow DocuColor dots in the scan decode to the machine's serial and confirm the time.
  • signed, but which original?provenance chain audit forensics Five signed checkpoints, only some of them genuinely valid. Finding the largest correct tree head scopes the log, and strict RFC 8785 and ordering checks single out the one redacted derivative that passes.
  • frost-ledgercustom copy-on-write page format forensics A volume with a stale format note and no walk subcommand. The real layout packs payloads against the end of each page with offsets counting backward, which lets the B+tree be walked by hand to an overflow chain holding the flag.
  • The Image That Forgottimeline normalisation and OCI resolution forensics Events in eight different UTC offsets must be normalised before the live instance can be identified. Resolving the OCI index to the right platform manifest then selects the key version that decrypts the incident note.
  • the last committed messageSQLite WAL multi-generation replay forensics The target operation was overwritten in the final database but survives in an older WAL generation. Replaying generations commit by commit and snapshotting at each boundary recovers the note and its attachment record.
  • last packetdebug header leak in a pcap forensics A developer left X-Debug-Mode on against an internal API, and the verbose reply carried an internal_key. It decodes through hex, base64 and a single-byte XOR whose key the known flag prefix gives away.
  • Write-HoleRAID5 write hole and AEAD recovery forensics Three raw drive images from a crashed NAS. Using e2fsck as the oracle recovers the RAID geometry, and the one record whose GCM tag was destroyed is decrypted independently of the tag.
  • Journal Necromancydeleted NTFS data clusters forensics The journal is wiped, but the data clusters of deleted files survive. Three tagged key shares are carved out by magic bytes, and the AES-GCM tag settles which byte layout is correct.
  • Phantom MeshCPA on a software-AES mesh forensics The rogue node's AES current draw modulates its transmitter bias, so the I/Q envelope is a power trace. Correlation power analysis recovers the fleet-wide link key, which unwraps the network key and the diagnostic report.
  • end-markSQLite WAL snapshot isolation forensics A trace records reader and writer order but not the bytes returned. Working out which WAL snapshot each deferred reader pinned, including the stale generation left by a RESTART checkpoint, reconstructs all 24 reads.
  • attestationtar PAX smuggling and referrer confusion web An intake filter checks only the raw USTAR name while the opening desk honours a PAX path override. Smuggling the release record through that gap and building a two-child index launders a sealed case into a live one.
  • Canonical SinCBOR canonicalization differential web Two byte-level filters guard the admin claim. An indefinite-length string slips past the raw blocklist and tag 55799 slips past the semantic one, and the final decoder still reads role as admin.
  • DraftpadCRDT range redaction leak web Redaction is computed from block index positions at request time while the content is always shipped. Merges shift indices, so the private window drifts off the flag block and the public export prints it.
  • The Hidden Test CaseUNION SQL injection web A lone quote returns 400 but a balanced payload returns every row, so the search is injectable. Four columns and a read of sqlite_master expose an internal_notes table holding the flag.
  • The Meridianprototype pollution to derived key web A static museum app hides a sealed vault behind a polluted shared-view link. Pollution through a unicode-escaped __proto__ key, plus the right pepper and a registrar audit, derives the AEAD key.
  • tearingTOCTOU race on shared state pwn The vault's win guard is checked separately from the update and shared across every connection. A burst of 64 threads makes many claims land inside the window at once.
  • mirageblind format string, no binary pwn No binary is shipped, only an echo service that leaks more than it should. Leaking stack words proves a static non-PIE image, and a positional arbitrary read pulls the flag straight from memory.
  • slabslab allocator aliasing to code execution pwn A freed note's XOR-encoded free-list link is attacker-controlled, so a note can be made to alias the slot table. That gives arbitrary read and write, enough to dump the program and hijack a function pointer.
  • echochamber-userSSTI to RCE and a nonce-gated socket pwn Default credentials lead to a Jinja2 preview feature that renders unsanitised input. RCE as analyst exposes the sentinel source, so the time-based socket nonce can be computed and used to read the user flag.
  • echochamber-rootinherited CAP_DAC_READ_SEARCH pwn The sentinel process holds CAP_DAC_READ_SEARCH and passes it to anything it spawns. Running a command through its socket reads the root flag with no classic privesc needed.
  • KeyStone-UserSSTI and a forged TPM quote pwn The vault verifies a quote against an attestation key the client itself supplies and never performs credential activation. After SSTI for a foothold, any RSA key can pose as the TPM and be handed a leaked SSH key.
  • KeyStone-RootTOCTOU in a setuid measurement helper pwn The helper hashes and allowlists a path, sleeps 20 ms, then opens the same path again to execute. Flipping a symlink between the two opens runs the attacker payload as root.
  • PHANTOM-15802.15.4 CCM*, Reed-Solomon and CPA forensics Three radio monitors and lab measurements must be combined to recover a missed diagnostic record. Getting the CCM* nonce byte order right, RS erasure decoding and a jitter-aware CPA for the key each unlock one stage.
  • GHOST-13journal rollover, ECDSA nonce reuse, DTMF forensics Four artifacts chain together. A wrapped 16-bit journal picks the active config, two Modbus signatures reuse an ECDSA nonce to leak the scalar, and DTMF tones from a WAV finish the key for the OTA unseal.
  • Fluxgate G3composing signed firmware parts pwn Each firmware artifact is verified alone but never as a set. One same-counter sidegrade adds an unlock opcode and a tab upgrade lowers its capability requirement, so a basic token can unlock.
  • FlaGuardshadow dex decoy and a missing flip() rev A shadow dex is meant to swap in a second flag checker, but the buffer is never flipped before loading, so the loader sees zero bytes. The visible checker is the real one, and it inverts byte by byte.
  • Mirror Garden1024 z3 leaves and native JNI rev 1024 generated Java leaves each encode a system of byte equations. All are solved with z3, one decodes differently, and Unicorn emulation of the native bloom() finishes the transform without reversing it by hand.
  • Faraday Cagepermuted-handler custom cipher rev The native checker is a bytecode VM whose 48 handlers are shuffled at runtime. Rebuilding the ISA in Python, validated against Unicorn, exposes a custom AES-like cipher that inverts by hand.
  • OatmealART OAT is authoritative over the APK rev The APK's notes are a staging decoy and the device actually ran the odex. Its checkFlag_v2 bytecode VM reduces to one invertible constraint per byte, and an FNV-1a finaliser turns the secret into the flag.
  • the-aggregate-remembersfederated gradient inversion AI Per-round weight updates from a federated linear-regression client leak its data. Rank analysis across six reused checkpoints shows a batch of two, and the raw 16-byte records are recovered from the gradients.
  • the-shape-of-silencehidden ReLU network and a version trap AI Clustered probe points fit exact integer affine pieces, so the model is a ReLU net. Four captures belong to a different model version, and dropping them resolves nine units that predict the targets.
  • The Deletion Echoinverting a ridge-regression oracle AI Published means and variances leak the Hessian and label moments of each release. Ordering the snapshots and isolating the deletion step, then an integer MILP, names the deleted records and their labels.
