Cairn A 2^20-step isogeny walk, done fast

#The challenge

Rule sheet challenge files/RULES.md (and the author's design source, SAC2021 "Verifiable Isogeny Walks: Towards an Isogeny-based Postquantum VDF"): p = 2^256-2^32-977, GF(p^2) = GF(p)[i]/(i^2+1), j0 = 1728, T = 2^20, L = [2,3,5,7]. Step i: h = SHA256(LE32(a)||LE32(b)||BE8(i)||salt); l = L[h[0] mod 4]; f_sq = squarefree part of Phi_l(j,Y); j := (h[1] mod n)-th smallest root of f_sq in GF(p^2). Then key = SHA256(ser(j_T)||"IsogenyLabyrinth") and the flag is AES-256-GCM.

This is a VDF by construction: 2^20 sequential root extractions, and each state is a fresh 512-bit field element, so there is no lookahead, no cycle and no reuse. "One step at a time. Do not look back." is literal.

#Why naive solves are hopeless

Finding the roots of a degree-<=8 polynomial that splits over GF(p^2) by generic factorisation costs 20-130 ms/step with PARI / NTL / FLINT / Sage (f.roots() / factor()), i.e. 6-40 hours for 2^20 steps. Measured on this box: Sage 134 ms/step, PARI factor 29 ms, PARI Mod(T,N)^p 10 ms, FLINT factor 7-13 ms, NTL CanZass 14 ms.

#The fast method: one exponentiation to p per step

  1. N = f*conj(f) in GF(p)[Y] is just (Re f)^2 + (Im f)^2 (no GF(p^2) arithmetic needed). Its roots are the roots of f and their conjugates, so every irreducible factor of Nsf = radical(N) has degree 1 or 2.
  2. A = Y^p mod Nsf - the whole cost of the step (256 squarings of deg-<=16 polys).
  3. G1 = gcd(Nsf, A-Y) are the F_p-rational factors; H1 = Nsf/G1 is a product of distinct irreducible quadratics Y^2 - t_j Y + n_j, because Y^p == conj(z) == t_j - Y in the component of a conjugate pair.
  4. T = A + Y mod H1 is therefore F_p-valued with value t_j in component j. Its values (the traces) are the roots of a degree-m (m <= 8) polynomial Psi over F_p, recovered with Newton's identities from sum_j t_j^k = Tr_{alg}(T^k)/2, where Tr is the trace of multiplication-by-x on F_p[Y]/H1 (an m x m matrix, cheap).
  5. Split H1 by gcd(H1, T - t) for each root t of Psi (roots of a split poly over F_p by Cantor-Zassenhaus/EDF with one small pow_mod per level).
  6. Roots of Y^2 - tY + n are (t +- sqrt(t^2-4n))/2. Every element of F_p is a square in GF(p^2), and s = disc^((p+1)/4) gives s^2 = +disc (QR, sqrt is s) or -disc (non-QR, sqrt is s*i) - exactly one exponentiation, no Legendre test.
  7. Keep the candidates with f(candidate) == 0 (this discards the conjugate roots), sort by (a,b) as unsigned 256-bit pairs, take index h[1] mod n.

Two further savings, both exact:

  • The previous vertex is a root of Phi_l(j,.) only when the degree did not change (the supplied Phi_l are not symmetric in X,Y - verified - so the backward edge exists only for the same l). It is deflated out whenever Phi_l(j,j_prev) == 0, which cuts the modulus degree by 2 for ~25% of steps.
  • f_sq is only recomputed (via gcd(f,f')) when the number of distinct roots found differs from deg f; normally f is already squarefree.

#Verification

  • Root sets per step identical to an independent Sage implementation (ref_roots.py) for the first 25 steps, with and without the deflation path - diff clean. That cross-checks norm/radical/Frobenius/trace-Newton/quadratic extraction end to end.
  • 143 returned roots over 30 steps: each satisfies f_sq(root) = 0, count equals deg f_sq and the number of distinct roots, list is sorted.
  • The final flag is gated by the AES-GCM tag: a wrong j_T cannot produce a flag. It was re-derived and decrypted with a second library (python cryptography AESGCM) as well as pycryptodome, same plaintext, format hiCTF{...}.
  • The walk ran 1,048,576 steps with no assertion failure (the solver raises rather than continuing on any internal inconsistency).

#Pitfalls worth remembering

  • cypari2 has no deriv; pari.Polrev vs pari.Pol (ascending vs descending) and PARI factor over a modulus needs the Mod(1,p) factor or it factors over Z.
  • FLINT fmpz_mod_poly gcd/divmod/radical/pow_mod/factor are the useful entry points from python (python-flint, installed for the system python3.14).
  • The Re/Im-gcd shortcut ("F_p-rational roots = gcd(Re f, Im f)") is WRONG in general: fsq(z)=0 gives Re(z) = -i*Im(z), not Re(z)=Im(z)=0. The norm-based route (radical(f*conj(f))) is the correct one.
