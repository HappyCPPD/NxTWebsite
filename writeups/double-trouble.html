Double Trouble shared RSA prime and an XOR key

crypto
flag OWASP{soul_stone_claimed}

#1. Challenge

Two RSA-encrypted messages. Two public keys. Something went wrong. Recover the flag.

The handout (challenge.txt) gives two (n, e=65537, c) triples:

The title and "something went wrong" strongly hint at broken key generation, and the "two keys" framing points at once class of bug: a shared prime between moduli (poor randomness / same keygen seed reused).

#2. Recon: factor with gcd

Two RSA moduli produced with a common prime p satisfy gcd(n1, n2) = p > 1. This is the read-only first move for any multi-key RSA challenge - it costs nothing and either finds the factor instantly or rules the class out.

It's > 1, so both moduli are broken. No need to factor anything else.

#3. Decrypt both ciphertexts

With p known:

Results:

Key Plaintext (raw)
1 CBYMHAI6NC44Iw0yMy4jKg0iKyAkIjclOg==
2 GAMORA

Key 1 is clearly base64. Key 2 is a short ASCII word - "GAMORA" (a Marvel Guardians name, and the challenge is flagged OWASP-style) - which is exactly the shape of a one-time-pad / XOR key.

That is the "something went wrong" of the title: the same secret material (GAMORA) leaked as one plaintext and was reused as the XOR key for the other message - a two-time-pad style failure.

#4. Recover the flag

Flag: OWASP{soul_stone_claimed}

#5. Reproduce

Stdlib only. Deterministic, single-shot.

#6. Why it works / lessons

  • Always try gcd(n1, n2) first on multi-key RSA - shared primes are the cheapest break.
  • A decryptable short plaintext that looks like a word is often the key to another layer (here XOR). Read plaintexts as data, not just as candidate flags.
  • "Something went wrong" + multiple public keys ⇒ keygen/randomness bug, not padding .
