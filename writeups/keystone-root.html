KeyStone-Root TOCTOU in a setuid measurement helper

pwn
flag isfcr{a_pcr_is_a_promise_not_proof}

Root half of KeyStone, building on the tpm-admin foothold from the User stage (SSTI RCE → forged quote → leaked SSH key).

A pre-staged ~/race directory holds safe (copy of /bin/true, allowlisted hash), evil (a payload dropping a setuid-root bash), and a flippable symlink link -> evil.

The bug: decompiling /usr/sbin/measure-and-run shows it (1) opens argv[1] and SHA256s its contents, (2) extends PCR 23 with that hash, (3) checks the hash against /etc/measure/allowed-hashes.txt, (4) on match, sleeps 20 ms, then (5) re-opens argv[1] by path a second time and fexecves it. Steps 1 and 5 open the same path twice with a fixed gap - a textbook TOCTOU. If the symlink points at safe during the hash/allowlist check but at evil during the re-open, the allowlisted hash is approved while the attacker payload executes as root.

Won after 10 attempts.

Root cause and fix: open once, verify and fexecve the same fd - never re-resolve the path, and drop the artificial sleep. Takeaway (the flag itself): extending/checking a PCR value proves nothing about what actually runs after the measurement when the measured file can be swapped between check and use - the measurement must be bound to the executed object (fd), not the path.
