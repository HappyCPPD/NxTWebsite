#1. How the input is consumed

At 0x4003eb - 0x400461 the 28 bytes on the stack are packed into a 256-bit integer at [rsp+0x20] (four 64-bit limbs):

i.e. k = int.from_bytes(input, 'little') - a 224-bit scalar (bits 224..255 are zero).

Then a loop runs ebx from 255 down to 0, testing bit ebx of k (bt rax, rbx; jae ...) and taking one of two update paths.

#2. The two helper routines

Hand-decompiling the small functions:

  • 0x4007c0 - modular addition mod M. 256-bit add of two 4-limb numbers, then if result >= M: result -= M, where M is the constant at 0x401320.
  • 0x4008f0 - modular subtraction mod M. a - b, or a - b + M when a < b (the 0x40094d branch computes M - b then adds a via 0x4007c0).
  • 0x400860 - modular multiplication mod M. Classic left-to-right double-and-add over 256 bits: acc = acc + acc (doubling) then acc = acc + rbp when the current mask bit is set, all in the Z_M additive group of 0x4007c0. Net effect: out = a * mask mod M.

Two candidate roots → two candidate k values (they are k and −k mod M-1, matching V_k = V_{-k}). Exactly one lies in [0, 2^224) and yields printable ASCII in little-endian - that is the flag.

#7. Takeaway

The "recurrence" is a Lucas V-sequence; the -2 / -P constants and the (V_2n, V_2n+1, V_2n+2) ladder are its fingerprint. Because Q=1 the sequence collapses to α^n + α^{-n}, turning the puzzle into a discrete log, and the author conveniently chose a prime M with a fully smooth M-1 (the flag name p0hlig_h3llman_sm00th gives it away). The rest is plumbing: Tonelli - Shanks for the two square roots, Pohlig - Hellman + CRT for the log, and a little-endian byte reinterpretation to read off the ASCII flag.
