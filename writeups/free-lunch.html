free-lunch CICO on a cube-root permutation

Flag: isfcr{b4ckw4rds_1s_h4lf_th3_d3gr33}

#The problem

An arithmetization-oriented style permutation on GF(p)^2, p = 2 (mod 3):

cbrt(v) = v^e, 3e = 1 mod (p-1), so cubing is a bijection and the round map is invertible. CICO: find x such that P(x, 0) has second output coordinate 0. The flag is AES-GCM sealed under SHA256(str(min_solution)).

#Key observation: flatten the chain

Let x_i denote the first coordinate after round i, with the starting pair (x_0, y_0) = (x, 0). Because y_{i+1} = x_i, the round relation x_{i+1} = cbrt(x_i + y_i + c_i) becomes a scalar 3-term recurrence:

which can be solved in either direction for free:

The CICO constraint P(x,0)[1] = 0 is exactly y_20 = x_19 = 0, and the start gives x_{-1} = 0. Two "boundary" coordinates pin the chain.

#What the author does (and we replicate)

Encode x_{i-1} as a polynomial in the final coordinate t = x_20, imposed x_19 = 0:

Then F = P[-1] is the polynomial whose roots t are the values of x_20 consistent with both boundary conditions. F = 0 is the CICO condition on the start x_{-1}=0.

The forward map raises degree by ~3 per round, but the backward recurrence (the cube is the expensive direction in the forward direction - hence the "no free lunch" title) only triples degree every two steps:

so deg F = 3^10 = 59049 - tiny. The whole CICO reduces to finding the GF(p) roots of a degree-59049 polynomial:

Because p = 2 mod 3 the cube root is a bijection, so there is no branching to manage; the gcd came out of degree 2, i.e. 2 CICO solutions ("twenty cube roots" in the blurb, but only two consistent preimages). We then walk the chain back to x_0 from each t-root, keep the ones landing at x_{-1}=0 with P(x_0,0)[1]=0, and take the smallest.

#Reproduce

Requires python-flint (fast nmod_poly) and pycryptodome:

Runs in ~6 s; deg F = 59049, deg gcd = 2. The bit that makes it fast is doing the polynomial recursion backwards (cube only every other round) and using FLINT's pow_mod for x^p mod F.

#Lesson

"Trivial to evaluate, huge forward degree" is a trap only if you attack the forward direction. The cube-root round is cheap to invert; expressing the condition in the inverse recurrence collapses the degree from ~3^19 to ~3^10 and makes the CICO a small root-finding problem.
