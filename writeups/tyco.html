So t1 and t2 are the e-th powers of two related messages (r and r + d) with the same e and modulus - textbook Franklin - Reiter.

#Method

Franklin - Reiter related-message attack: over Z/N[x],

The gcd of the two degree-e = 56263 polynomials collapses to the linear factor x - r; the constant term gives r, and r.to_bytes() decodes the flag. The Euclidean gcd runs ~35 000 of 56 263 steps (a few minutes).

#Verification

Structural: pow(r, e, N) == t1 and pow(r + d, e, N) == t2 both hold - the recovered r is exactly the plaintext the generator used.

#Notes

  • A 16-bit prime e with r tiny in bytes is the whole point: the flag is far below N, so r is the small root and FR recovers it directly.
